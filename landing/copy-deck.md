# Rekalla — landing page copy

Written for: live App Store app, cold Facebook/Instagram traffic and warm Google Ads traffic.

Two things to know before reading:

**No proof is invented.** You said the real users are family and friends, so every
testimonial slot below is a placeholder marked `[REAL QUOTE]`. Fill them with things
people actually said, in their own words, or delete the slot. One true sentence from
a real person beats five polished fakes, and fake reviews on an App Store product are
a legal problem as well as a trust one.

**Urgency is deliberately absent.** The usual countdowns and "act now" pressure are
the exact signals scam pages use, and your readers have been trained by their banks
and their children to distrust them. Reassurance is doing the persuading instead.
That is a considered swap, not an omission.

---

## 0. Two heroes, one page

You are running both Facebook and Google. Those readers are not in the same state of
mind, and one headline cannot serve both well:

- **Google** — already searching for this. Match the intent and get out of the way.
- **Facebook** — was not thinking about it at all, and is probably the adult child
  rather than the parent. The problem has to be made vivid before the product means
  anything.

Run the same page with a swapped hero block. Everything from the Lead Section down is
shared.

## 2. Lead Section

**What we do**
Rekalla turns paper into reminders. Photograph a calendar, an appointment card or a
bill, and the dates on it land on a calendar in your pocket.

**Pain point**
The appointment card goes in a drawer. The bill goes under the fruit bowl. The
calendar on the wall works beautifully, right up until you leave the kitchen. Nothing
is disorganised, exactly. It's just that none of it is with you when it matters.

**Solution teaser**
So don't reorganise any of it. Take a photo of what you already have, check what
Rekalla read, and let your phone do the remembering.

## 5. Power Differentiators

**Headline**
Why this works when other apps didn't

**Bullets**
- **You don't type.** Every other calendar app asks you to enter the appointment. That
  is the exact step that stops people, and it's the step Rekalla removes
- **It reads handwriting**, not just printed cards and bills
- **You approve every date.** Nothing is saved until you've seen it, so the app can
  never quietly get it wrong
- **Built for one person.** No shared accounts, no family dashboard, nobody looking
  over your shoulder
- **Big text by default.** Not an accessibility setting buried in a menu
- **No advertising, no tracking, nothing sold on**
- **Not a medical app**, and deliberately so. It won't touch prescriptions
- **Free to start**, with no card and no trial countdown

**Comparison table**

## 10. FAQs

**Headline**
Questions people ask first

- **Will it read my handwriting?**
  Usually, yes, if it's reasonably clear. Lay the page flat in good light. When it
  can't read something, it says so instead of guessing.

- **What if it gets a date wrong?**
  You see everything before it's saved, and you can change or delete anything
  afterwards. Nothing is ever stuck.

- **Who can see my calendar?**
  Only you. There are no shared accounts and no family dashboard. Not us either.

- **Do I have to type anything?**
  No. That's the whole point of it. You take the photo.

- **Is this a medical app?**
  No. Rekalla is not a medical device, gives no medical advice, and will not read
  prescriptions or medication lists. It's for everyday paper.

- **What does it cost?**
  Free to download and free to start. If you scan a great deal there'll be a paid plan
  later, and we'll tell you the price plainly before anything changes.

- **What if I don't get on with it?**
  Delete the app, and delete your account from Settings. Everything in it goes too.

## Notes for implementation

**The waitlist has to go.** The live page still says "Be first to know when Rekalla
launches" and collects emails. The app is out. Every CTA on the page should be the
App Store link (`https://apps.apple.com/app/id6794918254`). Keep the `waitlist` table
and its rows; just stop collecting.

**Run the two heroes as separate URLs** so the ad promise matches the page. Same page
otherwise. Point the Facebook ad at the version B hero and Google at version A.

**What to measure first**: scroll depth past the hero, and clicks on the App Store
button. If people scroll but don't click, the problem is the offer. If they don't
scroll, it's the headline.

**Before the page goes live**, `landing/privacy/` and `landing/terms/` still describe
caregivers, wellness check-ins and the Memory Vault. They describe a product you no
longer ship, and they're linked from the App Store listing.
